登廬山

Ascending Mount Lu

體式：七言律詩Regulated verse in seven-syllable lines (qilü)

小序The Poet’s Preface

禹疏九江，周匡飛仙，司馬遷南登廬山，思之不免神往

Yu channeled the Nine Rivers; Zhou Kuang ascended to immortality; Sima Qian climbed Mount Lu from the south—thinking on these, my spirit cannot but rise in longing.

原詩The Poem
結廬高遠向層巔，雲繞東南自古傳。九水禹疏誰著史，一湖日照我登山。朝攀撥霧仰峰險，暮宿和衣滴露寒。修道方知非本願，人間浪跡勝飛仙。
作者英譯The Poet’s Own Translation
I climb toward a high retreat among layered peaks;clouds have wreathed the southeast since ancient days.Who set down the story of Yu channeling the Nine Rivers?Sun strikes the whole lake as I ascend.At dawn I part the mist and look up to sheer summits;at dusk I sleep in my clothes, chilled by falling dew.Only in seeking the Way do I learn it was never my wish:to roam the human world is better than flying among immortals.
註釋Annotation
  1. 結廬陶潛《飲酒》其五：「結廬在人境，而無車馬喧。」按廬山得名，舊說即因周時匡俗兄弟結廬山中、受道成仙，廬存而人去，故曰廬山、匡廬；見《後漢書·郡國志》劉昭注引《豫章舊志》、慧遠《廬山記略》。起句用「結廬」二字，既是陶公的舊語，又暗合此山命名之由。
  2. 九水禹疏小序「禹疏九江」所本。《尚書·禹貢》：「九江孔殷。」《史記·河渠書》太史公曰：「余南登廬山，觀禹疏九江。」九江所指，漢唐以來聚訟不決（一說洞庭諸水，一說潯陽九派），故此句下一「誰著史」。
  3. 司馬遷南登廬山小序所舉第三事，即《河渠書》上引之語。太史公此行，是廬山見於正史之始；本篇頷聯的「著史」二字，正從這一句裡來。
  4. 周匡飛仙小序所舉第二事。匡俗，一作匡續、匡裕，或系之周，或系之漢初，史無定說；其兄弟七人結廬受道而去，為廬山最早的傳說。
  5. 一湖鄱陽湖，在廬山東南。「九水」與「一湖」、「誰著史」與「我登山」，一聯之內四處相校：四千年的疑案對這一日的實事，懸空的主語對落實的主語。
  6. 和衣滴露《詩·召南·行露》：「厭浥行露，豈不夙夜？謂行多露。」古以「行露」寫夜行沾溼之苦。此寫宿于山中不解衣而臥，一夜為露所滴。
  7. 修道方知非本願與小序「思之不免神往」正相反。神往在前，識非本願在後；「方知」二字尤要緊——不是本來不願，是登過、宿過、冷過之後才知道不願。
  8. 人間浪跡勝飛仙反用李白《廬山謠寄盧侍御虛舟》之結：「早服還丹無世情，琴心三疊道初成……先期汗漫九垓上，願接盧敖遊太清。」李白結在飛昇，此篇結在不飛。小序中神往的匡俗，亦正是飛昇的那一位；一篇之中兩個飛昇的先例俱在，而俱被辭謝。
鑑賞Appreciation

Prefatory Note

Mount Lu has long drawn seekers—emperors, historians, hermits, poets—some pursuing immortality, others seeking history or themselves. This poem begins as another ascent but concludes with an unexpected decision.

Across the Centuries

Sima Qian: "I climbed south to Mount Lu and saw where Yu had guided the Nine Rivers."

Wen Ren: "Who set down the story of Yu channeling the Nine Rivers? Sun strikes the whole lake as I ascend."

Historical & Philosophical Notes

Sima Qian (司馬遷, c. 145–86 BCE): Han dynasty historian and author of the Records of the Grand Historian. In its "Treatise on Rivers and Waterways," he recorded climbing Mount Lu and viewing the region traditionally associated with Yu the Great's regulation of the Nine Rivers.

Yu the Great (大禹): Legendary sage-king credited with controlling great floods by dredging and guiding China's waterways. Mount Lu associates with his regulation of the Nine Rivers.

Afterword

The poem answers with quiet reflection rather than proclamation. "Mist, height, wet clothes—the plain furniture of an ordinary climb—replace the immortals and historians." The final lines pivot unexpectedly: the speaker realizes spiritual seeking was never his true longing. Instead, he discovers that “to roam the human world is better than flying among immortals.”

Listening Companions:

China: 醉漁唱晚 (Drunken Fisherman Sings at Dusk)—its swaying rhythm echoes the climb and the choice of mortal wandering.

Britain: Vaughan Williams' The Lark Ascending—its solitary rising line mirrors the ascent, while its unresolved ending matches the poem's discovery found not at the summit but in continuing the journey.

七律的格局在此詩裡用得很老實：起聯點地，頷聯入史，頸聯寫身，尾聯翻案。老實之中，最見功夫的是頷聯的「誰」與「我」。「九水禹疏誰著史，一湖日照我登山」——上句問的是四千年，下句答的是這一天；上句的主語懸空，下句的主語落實。兩個字對峙，把一部治水的正史與一個人的一次登山擱在同一副天平上，而詩人並不宣布哪一邊更重。
頸聯最不像詩，也最像登山：「朝攀撥霧仰峰險，暮宿和衣滴露寒。」攀、撥、仰、宿四個動作都不帶情緒；一個「和衣」，把仙山寫成了行旅。凡寫廬山者多寫其奇——李白寫「飛流直下三千尺」，蘇軾寫「不識廬山真面目」，此聯偏寫其累。有這一聯的鈍，尾聯的翻案才不顯得輕巧：說「人間勝過飛仙」的，是一個昨夜和衣捱過冷的人，不是一個在山下想象的人。
末句是全詩的賭注。李白同遊此山，結在「先期汗漫九垓上，願接盧敖遊太清」；小序裡令他神往的匡俗，也正是飛昇的那一位。此詩把兩個飛昇的先例都擺出來，然後當面辭謝。妙在辭得不激烈——只用一個「勝」字作比較，不用一個「非」字下駁斥；「修道方知非本願」也只說「非本願」，不說「不可求」。全篇沒有和神仙爭辯，只是不去了。
此意在中國詩裡其實是罕見的。謝靈運登山而求「理」，李白登山而求「仙」，蘇軾登山而求「悟」，都仍是有所求；此詩登了、宿了、冷了，最後所得是「不必求」。以七律之整齊承一個近乎散淡的結論，格律的框子與結論的松，恰好互相牽制，所以既不顯得潦草，也不顯得說教。

The frame of this lüshi is used with deliberate plainness: the opening couplet places the poet, the second turns to history, the third to the body, the last overturns the whole expedition. Within that plainness the sharpest stroke is the pair of pronouns in the second couplet — “Who set down the story of Yu channeling the Nine Rivers? Sun strikes the whole lake as I ascend.” The first line asks about four thousand years and leaves its subject hanging; the second answers with a single day and names its subject. Two syllables set a dynasty-founding labour and one man’s morning walk on the same scales, and the poem declines to say which side goes down.
The third couplet is the least poetic and the most like actual climbing: climb, part, gaze, sleep — four verbs, none of them coloured — and then the detail that gives the poem its weather, sleeping in one’s clothes and waking wet. Most poets of Mount Lu write its strangeness; Li Bai the cataract falling three thousand feet, Su Shi the face one cannot see for standing in it. This one writes the fatigue. Because of that dull couplet the reversal at the end carries: the man who says the human world is better than immortality is a man who was cold all night, not a man imagining the mountain from below.
The last line is the wager. Li Bai’s poem on this same mountain ends by soaring off into the empyrean with Lu Ao; Zhou Kuang, the recluse whose longing sets the prose preface in motion, is himself one of the ascended. Both precedents are laid out, and both are politely declined. The refusal is not loud: one comparative — better than — does the whole work, and the line before it says only it was never my wish, not it cannot be had. Nobody argues with the immortals here. The poet simply does not go.
That ending is rarer in Chinese poetry than it looks. Xie Lingyun climbs for a principle, Li Bai for transcendence, Su Shi for insight; all still climb toward something. This poem climbs, sleeps out, gets cold, and comes down with the conclusion that there was nothing up there it needed. Carrying so unstrenuous a conclusion inside so strict a form is what keeps it from sounding either careless or preachy: the tight frame holds the loose thought in place.

西方回聲A Western Echo
跨文化延伸閱讀Further reading across traditions · optional

這些並讀旨在提出問題，不代表作品等量齊觀、存在直接影響或獲得他人背書。These juxtapositions invite questions, not claims of equivalence, direct influence or endorsement.

On 26 April 1336 Petrarch climbed Mont Ventoux — by his own account for no reason but to see what was at the top, which was itself a novelty. He reached the summit, was overwhelmed by the view, opened the Confessions at random, and read Augustine on men who go to admire mountains and rivers and pass themselves by. He came down having decided the ascent had been about something other than the ascent. The letter (Familiares IV.1) is the standard Western instance of a climb that ends by turning away from its own summit — the same movement as this poem’s last couplet, and reached, in both cases, only on the way down. Where Petrarch turns inward, Wen Ren turns outward: not to the soul but to renjian, the world of the living. It is the identical structure with the opposite exit.按：彼特拉克於一三三六年四月二十六日登法國旺圖山，自云無他故，只為看看山頂有什麼。既登絕頂，為大觀所奪，隨手翻開隨身的奧古斯丁《懺悔錄》，所遇正是「人往觀山川之高、海波之闊，而遺其自身」一節，遂廢然而返，以為此行所求本不在山頂。其書見《書信集》第四卷第一通，為西方「登而後辭其頂」之祖。與本篇尾聯同一機杼，且同樣是在下山路上才想明白的。所異者，彼特拉克轉而向內，歸於靈魂；文刃轉而向外，歸於人間。結構全同，出口正相反。
